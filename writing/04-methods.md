### 3.1 Problem formulation and design requirements

Let $I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}} \in [0,1]^{1\times H\times W}$ be a registered infrared–visible pair, the visible image reduced to its luminance (Y) channel. The backbone, S4Fusion [S4Fusion, TIP 2025], encodes both modalities with selective state-space (SSM) encoders, fuses them at multiple scales in bidirectional-scan fusion blocks, and decodes a fused luminance image $Y = f_\theta(I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}})$. The question this section formalizes is where, in such a pipeline, the decision should be made when the two modalities disagree about the same physical structure.

We define cross-modal conflict at a pixel as the conjunction of two conditions: both modalities carry structure there (both gradient magnitudes non-negligible) and their gradient vectors disagree in orientation or polarity — the common case at modality boundaries, where a thermal edge and its visible counterpart often have opposite polarity. In this regime every convex blend is structurally destructive. If locally $\nabla I_{\mathrm{vi}} \approx -\nabla I_{\mathrm{ir}}$ with common magnitude $g$, the blend $F = w\,I_{\mathrm{ir}} + (1-w)\,I_{\mathrm{vi}}$ with $w \in [0,1]$ has gradient magnitude $|\nabla F| \approx |2w-1|\,g$: it cancels the structure completely at $w = 1/2$ and preserves it only at the endpoints $w \in \{0,1\}$ — that is, only under full commitment to one modality. A soft weighting, in image space or in feature space, cannot preserve a conflicting boundary; it can only choose how much of it to cancel. A per-pixel weight field is also the wrong instrument for a second reason: optimized over the whole image, it has no pressure to saturate exactly at the pixels where blending fails.

The natural alternative — giving the operator more capacity to represent boundaries — we tested directly and rejected: a pre-registered, same-budget falsification (the H1 arm of Section 3.4) showed that modulating the SSM state dynamics (the $B$, $C$, $\Delta$ parameters) with a learned boundary-reliability map does not improve structural fusion metrics (frozen verdict in Section 5). The bottleneck is therefore not state capacity — the state-capacity dilution account does not transfer to structural gains — but the absence of an arbitration decision at conflicting pixels.

These observations induce three design requirements. (R1) *Image-space operation*: which modality to keep at a pixel is defined and observable in image space, and after the falsification above there is no evidence that intervening in feature space transfers to structural gains; image-space operation also leaves the backbone's internals untouched. (R2) *Conflict-triggered selectivity*: the mechanism must act only where the modalities actually conflict, so non-conflict pixels retain the pretrained model's behaviour exactly and any measured gain is attributable to arbitration rather than to a global perturbation. (R3) *Per-pixel commitment behind a zero-initialized gate*: at a conflict pixel the output should move toward the locally salient modality rather than a blend of both, and the whole mechanism must start as an exact identity so that pretrained weights — including the official public checkpoint — remain valid. We refer to the resulting mechanism class as image-space per-pixel commitment at conflict pixels: an arbitration added on top of a fusion backbone, not a re-weighting inside it.

### 3.2 Conflict-Gated Arbitration

Conflict-Gated Arbitration (CGA) implements the three requirements as one image-space module: detect conflict, form a per-pixel commitment toward the salient modality, and apply that commitment through a zero-initialized gate only in conflict regions. It is attached once, at the output of the S4Fusion decoder: the augmented forward pass retains the original inputs at native resolution and applies CGA to $(I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}}, Y)$, returning the arbitrated image $F$; encoders, fusion blocks and decoder are untouched, and the interface (two source images plus a fused image) is backbone-agnostic in principle <!-- src: code/S4Fusion.py VSSM.forward (ir0/vi0 retained; cga applied after final reconstruction conv) -->.

**Conflict signal.** For each modality $m \in \{\mathrm{ir}, \mathrm{vi}\}$, fixed $3\times 3$ Sobel kernels scaled by $1/4$ give gradient components $G_x^m, G_y^m$ and magnitude

$$g_m = \sqrt{(G_x^m)^2 + (G_y^m)^2 + \varepsilon}, \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-6},$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py _sobel (kernels /4), conflict_signal (eps=1e-6) — design constants -->

and orientation agreement between the two gradient fields is their cosine

$$c = \frac{G_x^{\mathrm{ir}} G_x^{\mathrm{vi}} + G_y^{\mathrm{ir}} G_y^{\mathrm{vi}}}{g_{\mathrm{ir}}\, g_{\mathrm{vi}} + \varepsilon} \in [-1, 1].$$

The conflict prior is the closed form

$$\kappa = \min\!\left(g_{\mathrm{ir}}, g_{\mathrm{vi}}\right)\, \frac{1 - c}{2}.$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py conflict_signal -->

The minimum makes the signal symmetric: conflict requires structure in *both* modalities — a pixel where only one modality has an edge contributes exactly zero — while $(1-c)/2$ maps aligned gradients ($c \approx 1$) to $\kappa \approx 0$ and opposite polarity ($c \approx -1$) to its maximum. The prior is computed in fp32 even under mixed precision, so that the square-root epsilon remains effective, and is normalized per image by its spatial maximum and clipped, $\hat\kappa = \mathrm{clip}\big(\kappa / (\max_u \kappa + \epsilon);\, 0, 1\big)$, serving as a self-supervised target below <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py conflict_signal (.float() fp32 note; amax normalization + clamp) -->. No annotation enters any of these expressions; the signal is derived from the input pair alone.

**Learned conflict map.** The prior is pointwise and fixed; the mechanism learns its conflict map from richer evidence. A convolutional head $h_c$ (a $3\times 3$ convolution from 5 to 16 channels, SiLU, a depthwise $3\times 3$ convolution, SiLU, a $1\times 1$ convolution to one channel) receives the stack $[I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}}, g_{\mathrm{ir}}, g_{\mathrm{vi}}, \hat\kappa]$ and produces

$$M = \sigma\big(h_c([I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}}, g_{\mathrm{ir}}, g_{\mathrm{vi}}, \hat\kappa])\big) \in [0,1]^{1\times H\times W},$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py ConflictGatedArbitration.conflict_head (5→16 conv3x3, SiLU, dw conv3x3 groups=16, SiLU, 1x1→1; hidden=16) -->

a map that can absorb context the pointwise prior cannot — textured versus structural disagreement, for instance — while remaining grounded on $\hat\kappa$ through the loss below.

**Commit selection.** The commitment is a per-pixel convex selection between the two source images,

$$K = s\, I_{\mathrm{ir}} + (1-s)\, I_{\mathrm{vi}}, \qquad s = \sigma\big(h_s([I_{\mathrm{ir}}, I_{\mathrm{vi}}, g_{\mathrm{ir}}, g_{\mathrm{vi}}]) + 2p\big),$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py forward (select_head input 4ch; prior bias 2.0; commit = s*ir + (1-s)*vi) -->

where $h_s$ is a $3\times 3$ convolution (4 to 16 channels), SiLU, $1\times 1$ convolution stack and $p = 2\cdot\mathbb{1}[g_{\mathrm{ir}} > g_{\mathrm{vi}}] - 1 \in \{-1, +1\}$ names the modality with the larger local gradient. The final layer of $h_s$ is initialized to zero, so before training the selection reduces to $s = \sigma(2p) \approx 0.88$ toward that modality — the max-response commit identified by the training-free pre-check reported in the Introduction — and training may sharpen or override it <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py __init__ (zeros_ on select_head[-1] weight/bias); σ(2)≈0.88 is a mathematical consequence of this init, not an experimental number -->.

**Gate and combination.** A single scalar $\alpha$, zero-initialized, bounds the mechanism globally; the learned conflict map localizes it:

$$w = \tanh(\alpha)\cdot M, \qquad F = Y + w \odot (K - Y),$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py forward (gate_scale scalar zeros(1); weight = tanh(gate_scale)*conflict; fused_cga = fused + weight*(commit−fused)) -->

equivalently $F = (1-w) \odot Y + w \odot K$ with per-pixel $w \in [0, \tanh\alpha]$. Where $M \to 0$ the output equals the backbone's fusion $Y$ exactly; at conflict pixels it moves toward the commitment by at most $\tanh(\alpha)$. At initialization $\tanh(0) = 0$, hence $F \equiv Y$ at every pixel: the module is an exact identity before training.

**Training objective.** The backbone keeps the repository's standard fine-tuning objective — an intensity term ($\mathcal{L}_1$ to the pixelwise maximum of the inputs), a gradient term ($\mathcal{L}_1$ to the pixelwise maximum of the Sobel magnitudes), an edge-aware smoothness term, and a cross-resolution scale-consistency term — at repository defaults <!-- src: code/train_gates.py fusion_loss; code/train_brss.py compute_loss + defaults -->. CGA adds two terms. The conflict grounding term is a binary cross-entropy with logits between the head's logit and the detached target $\hat\kappa$ (numerically safe under mixed precision) <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py cga_conflict_loss (BCEWithLogits vs detached cf_n) -->. The conflict-gated commit term is

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{commit}} = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{u} \bar{M}(u)\,\big(F(u) - K(u)\big)^2, \qquad \bar{M} = \mathrm{detach}(M),$$ <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py cga_commit_loss (conflict_pred detached, clamp 0–1; mean over pixels) -->

which pushes the output toward the per-pixel commitment in predicted conflict regions only. Since $F - K = (1 - w)(Y - K)$, minimizing this term opens the gate and improves the selection simultaneously; it is the term that drives the zero-initialized gate away from zero. The total objective is

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fusion}} + \lambda_c\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{conflict}} + \lambda_k\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{commit}},$$

with $\lambda_c = 0.1$ and $\lambda_k = 10.0$ in all reported runs <!-- src: code/run_str_train.sh + code/run_seeds_queue_v2.sh (--w-cga-conflict 0.1 --w-cga-commit 10.0) -->.

### 3.3 Zero-initialized compatibility and training protocol

**Exact compatibility at load time.** The gate is the only path through which CGA can modify the output, and it is closed at initialization: $\tanh(0) = 0$ makes $w \equiv 0$ and $F \equiv Y$, regardless of the randomly initialized heads behind the closed gate. Loading the official S4Fusion checkpoint into the augmented architecture therefore reproduces the baseline exactly: every pretrained key loads unchanged, and the only keys absent from the checkpoint are those of the CGA module, all of which act behind the closed gate <!-- src: code/train_brss.py load_checkpoint (strict=False); code/logs/abl_CGA_str_s42.log records missing=CGA keys only, unexpected=0 (log fact, count not carried into text) -->. This is LayerScale-style compatibility [LayerScale, ICCV 2021] taken to its limit — the added mechanism is a strict no-op at load time — and it is what makes the study controlled: all arms of Section 3.4 start from the same public weights, so any measured difference is attributable to the intervention rather than to architecture surgery. After training, the gate saturates near unity in the reported checkpoint — the frozen value and its visualization are given in Section 5 — which excludes the self-falsification scenario in which the gate stays at zero and the measured gains have some other origin <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §4 (tanh(gate_scale)=0.9998); number deliberately not expanded here per writing-discipline rule 1 -->.

**Training protocol.** Every arm fine-tunes from the official public checkpoint under a unified schedule: $128\times 128$ random crops, batch size 1 with 8-step gradient accumulation, 2,048 crops per epoch drawn by dataset-balanced sampling from a multi-source training manifest, AdamW with learning rate $2\times 10^{-5}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, gradient-norm clipping at 10.0, and mixed-precision training on a 4 GB-class consumer GPU <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §6 (crop/batch/accum/samples/lr/amp); code/train_brss.py (wd 1e-4, clip 10.0, AMP/GradScaler); code/README_BRSS.md ("4 GB-class GPU") -->. The CGA arm — the pre-registered H5 re-test — extends the schedule from 20 to 32 epochs and assigns the CGA parameters a separate learning rate of $0.01$ with zero weight decay, so that the zero-initialized mechanism can open within a short fine-tuning budget while the backbone is preserved at the base rate <!-- src: code/run_str_train.sh (header: "extend 20 -> 32 epochs"; --gate-lr 0.01), code/run_seeds_queue_v2.sh; code/train_brss.py gate_lr param group (wd=0) -->. Three seeds (42, 123, 3407) are run for every reported cell; checkpoints are selected by lowest validation loss and written atomically <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §2–§3 (s42/s123/s3407); 机制消融实验协议.md §5; code/train_brss.py (_atomic_save; best-by-val) -->. No detection supervision enters training anywhere: the commit signal is self-derived from the input pair, and the detection endpoint of Section 3.4 is evaluation-only — the detector is never in the training loop.

**Baseline arm.** The same-budget baseline (B0) retrains the unmodified architecture from the same checkpoint under the unified 20-epoch schedule with all optional branches disabled, anchoring comparisons to the training regime rather than to published numbers <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §5 ("B0 系两种子 20 epoch 全程无 NaN"); 机制消融实验协议.md §3 (B0); code/run_seeds_queue_v2.sh (B0 --epochs 20, boundary-mode none, fusion-scales none) -->. The 32-epoch extension of the CGA arm was registered together with its success criteria before the re-test was run, with every other knob held identical <!-- src: code/run_str_train.sh header (pre-registered success criteria C1/C2/C3, "extend 20 -> 32 epochs, keep every other knob identical"); 机制消融实验协议.md §10 -->. A late-training validation-loss instability observed in some CGA seeds, with best checkpoints selected before its onset, is reported with its frozen record in Section 5 <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §5 (NaN record); no numbers expanded here -->.

### 3.4 Pre-registered falsification design

**Design principle.** The methodological claim of this paper rests on an ordering: hypotheses, pass criteria, and even the narrative disposition for each outcome tier were fixed before the experiments, and the verdicts are frozen <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §1 (H1–H5 pre-registered), §10 (A/B/C tier dispositions registered before the H5 re-test); GATE1_FREEZE 冻结声明 -->. Two hypothesis tracks share the public starting checkpoint. H1 tests the mechanism hypothesis — that boundary failures of SSM fusion originate in the state dynamics and can be repaired by feature-space modulation. H5 tests the arbitration hypothesis — that a conflict-gated, image-space commitment produces a detectable downstream gain. The tracks are read independently: a negative verdict on H1 demotes the state-capacity account without touching H5, and vice versa; neither can compensate for the other <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §1 ("两线独立读数,任一失败各自降级,不互相兜底") -->.

**H1 arm: feature-space state modulation.** Each fusion block is augmented with a boundary-reliability estimator, a small convolutional network over eight feature-structure channels (normalized Sobel magnitudes and absolute Laplacian responses per modality, their edge agreement, per-modality local contrast, and the edge-magnitude difference) plus a pooled two-channel global context, producing a three-way (IR / VI / shared) reliability distribution at every position; the estimator is initialized neutrally (uniform reliability) with weak fixed structural priors that the learned residual may override <!-- src: code/modules/boundary.py BoundaryReliabilityEstimator (8 descriptors + 2 global-context channels; zero-init last layer → softmax 1/3; logits + 0.1·prior) -->. Three tanh-bounded, zero-initialized scalars per block then modulate the selective scan. Writing the reliability maps $r_{\mathrm{ir}}, r_{\mathrm{vi}}, r_{\mathrm{sh}}$ and $s_i = \tanh(\beta_i)$ with $\beta$ zero-initialized,

$$\gamma_{\mathrm{ir}} = 1 + s_0\,(r_{\mathrm{ir}} - r_{\mathrm{vi}}), \qquad \gamma_{\mathrm{sh}} = 1 + s_1\, r_{\mathrm{sh}}, \qquad \gamma_{\Delta,m} = 1 + s_2\,(1 - r_m),$$

$$B_m \leftarrow \gamma_m\, B_m, \qquad C_m \leftarrow \gamma_m\, \gamma_{\mathrm{sh}}\, C_m, \qquad \Delta_m \leftarrow \gamma_{\Delta,m}\, \Delta_m,$$ <!-- src: code/modules/fusion.py forward_core (state modulation; boundary_state_scale zeros(3), tanh-bounded; shared gain also applied to the cross-modal "mark" component) -->

with the shared gain applied to a cross-modal component as well. The pre-registered pass criterion requires improvement in at least two structural metrics (SF, AG, QABF or EOR) under per-image paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction at $p < 0.05$; the frozen verdict is a falsification, reported in Section 5 <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §1 (H1 row); GATE1_FREEZE §3 (H1 证伪, frozen) -->.

**H5 endpoint.** The primary endpoint of the arbitration hypothesis is deliberately mechanism-external: detector recall on high-conflict objects of the full 300-image M3FD test split, with an off-the-shelf detector held fixed across all arms (configuration in Section 5) <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §1 ("M3FD full-300"); code/arb_detect_full300.py (fixed detector, no training-loop involvement) -->. An object's conflict score is the mean of the closed-form prior $\kappa$ (Section 3.2) over its ground-truth box, computed from the input pair alone; objects strictly above the 66th percentile of conflict scores form the high-conflict subset <!-- src: code/arb_full300_stats.py (per-object mean κ over GT box; cf > percentile 66) -->. The comparison is per-object and paired: recovered objects (missed by the baseline, detected with CGA) and lost objects (the converse) form the discordant pairs, assessed by an exact binomial (McNemar-style) test; the pre-registered pass criterion is $p < 0.05$ together with recovered $-$ lost $\geq 3$ <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §1 (H5 row); code/arb_full300_stats.py (binomtest on discordant pairs; gate-C1 logic) -->. Because the subset is defined by the same statistic that triggers the mechanism, two guards against selection bias are built in: the endpoint is a downstream quantity (detector recall, not any internal of the fusion), and the stricter gates below add all-object mAP50 and structural non-inferiority.

**Stricter gates and content controls.** Three stricter gates were registered alongside the primary endpoint: gate-C1 requires the high-conflict recall gain to reach $+0.02$; gate-C2 requires the all-object mAP50 to improve by at least $+0.01$; gate-C3 requires SF and AG to be non-inferior within a relative margin of $-1\%$ against the same-budget baseline <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §3 (gate-C1 ≥ +0.02; gate-C2 mAP50 ≥ +0.01; gate-C3 SF/AG 相对非劣性 ≥ −1%); code/run_str_train.sh header (criteria pre-registered); code/struct_noninferior.py -->. Inference-time content controls complete the design: the learned conflict map can be replaced at test time by an all-ones map (the mechanism applied everywhere) or by a fixed, seeded spatially permuted copy of itself (density preserved, placement destroyed), attributing any gain to correct placement rather than to intervention per se; the H1 arm carries the analogous control on its reliability map <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py _apply_conflict_override (uniform / shuffle, seeded permutation); code/modules/boundary.py _apply_override (analogous H3 control) -->. These hooks are part of the frozen module code, not post-hoc edits.

**Reporting discipline.** The protocol pre-registered the narrative disposition for each outcome tier before the H5 re-test, so that a weak result could not be reframed after the fact <!-- src: 机制消融实验协议.md §10 (A/B/C 档位, 预先登记) -->. Accordingly, the H5 outcome is reported with fixed wording — "statistically significant but below the stricter pre-registered gate thresholds" — never as having passed them; registered negative results (the H1 falsification, the negative distillation result) are reported as first-class findings in Section 5 <!-- src: GATE1_FREEZE §3 档位判定 (frozen wording); writing/00-PAPER-CONFIG.md 写作纪律合同 §2 -->.

### 3.5 Computational cost

CGA's learnable budget is confined to two small convolutional stacks (16-channel hidden width) and one scalar; the Sobel operators are fixed and learn nothing, and the module is attached at a single point at output resolution, so its cost does not scale with the backbone's depth <!-- src: code/modules/cga.py (qualitative statement only; exact count not in the frozen record) -->. Its footprint is selective rather than global: the applied weight is proportional to the conflict map, so a trained model perturbs the fused image only in conflict regions, and at load time the module is an exact no-op.
